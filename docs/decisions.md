## Details

### D2 — Rounding

- **Equal split:** each person gets `floor(total / n)`. The leftover minor units are given out one at a time, in a fixed order (order of joining the household). Example: ₹100.00 split 3 ways is 33.34, 33.33, 33.33.
- **Percentage split:** percentages are stored as basis points (whole numbers) that must total exactly 10000. They are converted to amounts with the largest-remainder method, so the shares add up exactly to the total.
- **Custom split:** amounts must add up exactly to the total, in minor units.

### D5 — Invitations

- **Token:** 32 random bytes. Only its SHA-256 hash is stored. The raw link is shown once, when it's created.
- **Defaults:** expires after 7 days; can be used at most 10 times; can be revoked.
- **Joining:** requires signing in. Expiry, use limit, and revocation are checked in one transaction.
- **Expired or revoked link message:** "This invite is no longer valid — ask a household member for a new one."

### D9 — Settlements

- **Who can record:** only the payer or the recipient. The other party gets an in-app notification.
- **No editing:** a settlement can only be voided (`voided_at`, `voided_by`) and recorded again. The payer, the recipient, or the owner can void it.
- **Partial amounts:** allowed. The app warns if the amount is more than what's currently owed.
- **Wording:** "A recorded a payment of ₹500 to B."
- **Notice:** every settlement surface shows "Recorded in the app — no money is moved."

How roadmap Phase 10 (2026-11-12) implements it:

- **Counts once recorded.** There's no pending state (see O10). A voided settlement stays listed and readable, with who voided it and when, but doesn't count. It can't be un-voided.
- **The warning.** The form shows what's owed and what the payment leaves, and its review step warns when the amount is more than what's owed. The database checks the latest figure as well. `record_settlement` refuses with `HJ017` until the caller acknowledges that exact figure ("Record anyway"), so the warning returns if the figure changes in between, for example when the other person recorded the same payment a moment earlier.
- **The notification** to the other party arrives with in-app notifications (Phase 17, D12). The `settlement_recorded` journal event it will use is written now.
- **Wording when the recipient records it:** "B recorded a payment of ₹500 from A."

### D26 — The household journal (proposed)

How roadmap Phase 13 implements it ([journal.md](journal.md)):

- **Who sees it:** active members only, enforced by RLS and by `household_journal` itself. The same
  journal for the owner and members (D3). No new access rule: this is how `activity_events` has been
  readable since Phase 6.
- **What an entry says:** who did it, what, when (in the household's timezone, D11), the expense's
  description and amount or the payment's people, amount and date, and for an edit what changed (D6).
  Payments use D9's wording and notice; there's no pending or completed state (O10 stays open).
- **What it leaves out:** payment notes, split shares and percentages, invite-link expiry and use
  limits, emails, tokens and ids. They're still stored with the events, unchanged.
- **Folded entries:** links revoked by `leave_household` or `remove_member` (their creator left) aren't
  separate entries. Revoking a link by hand is.

### D10 — Recurring bills

- **Patterns:** weekly, monthly, or yearly, repeating every N periods.
- **Short months:** a monthly bill due on the 29th–31st falls on the last day of shorter months.
- **No duplicates:** `unique(recurring_bill_id, recurrence_period)` on expenses.
- **Edits:** apply to future periods only. Expenses already recorded are not changed.
- **Disabling:** stops future reminders.

How roadmap Phase 14 (2026-11-13) implements it ([bills.md](bills.md)):

- **Patterns:** weekly, monthly or yearly, every 1 to 52 of them; the form names every week, every
  2 weeks, every month, every 3 months and every year. Each period is worked out from the first due
  date, so a bill on the 31st comes back to the 31st after a short month, and one on 29 February
  falls on the 28th in other years.
- **Reminders** are the bills page and the Household page, in the app. Nothing is sent: in-app
  notifications are D12's, in roadmap Phase 17.
- **No duplicates:** the unique index is on expenses that aren't deleted (D7), so deleting the
  expense reopens the period; restoring it then is refused if the period was recorded again or
  skipped. `recurrence_period` is the period's due date.
- **No scheduler** (ADR-010): periods are worked out on every read and stored only by the action
  that needs one.
- **Edits:** details and schedule are changed separately. Periods that have already come keep their
  dates and expected amount; a new schedule starts today or later.
- **Disabling** is pausing (or ending). Periods that have already come stay open until recorded or
  skipped.

### D27 — Recurring bills: who, skipping, pausing (proposed)

- **Who:** any active member adds a bill and records a period. The person who added it, the person
  who usually pays it, or the owner edits it, changes its schedule, pauses, resumes or ends it, and
  skips a period or undoes a skip. Nothing approved said; this follows D6 for expenses.
- **Skipping:** for a bill that didn't come this time. No expense, not paid, kept in the history;
  "Undo skip" opens the period again. A recorded period can't be skipped, and a skipped one can't be
  recorded until the skip is undone.
- **Pausing:** periods that have come stay open; none comes while paused; on resuming, the next
  period is the first on the schedule from today, and the periods that fell meanwhile never come.
- **Ending:** no period after today (or after one already paid early); a later last due date can
  be set again.
- **Early payment:** the next period can be recorded or skipped before it's due; nothing further
  ahead.
- **Due soon:** within 7 days of the due date (`DUE_SOON_DAYS` in `src/lib/bills/schedule.ts`).
- **Bounds:** a first due date within the past year and the next ten.

### D28 — Shopping list and chores (proposed)

How roadmap Phase 15 (2026-11-13) implements it ([shopping-chores.md](shopping-chores.md)). The
approved model (requirements-notes §3) gives items a name, a text quantity, who added and who bought
them, and chores a title, an assignee, a due date and who completed them. Nothing approved covered
the rest, so these are proposed:

- **One list:** items belong to the household; there is no list table and no second list.
- **Shopping rights:** any active member does everything on the list, including other people's
  items: it's a shared list.
- **Chore rights:** any member adds one. The person who added it, its assignee or the owner edits it
  (which is also how it's reassigned) or removes it (D6's pattern). Completing and reopening: the
  same people, or anyone when the chore is assigned to nobody (so anyone can pick it up) or to
  someone who has left.
- **Assignees** are an active member or nobody when chosen. Someone who leaves keeps their
  assignments, shown with "(left)" (D13); nothing is reassigned automatically, and they can't be
  chosen again once moved.
- **Removal, not deletion:** removed items and chores are kept with who removed them and when, so the
  journal still makes sense; they can't be changed or brought back.
- **Repeats:** buying, reopening, completing or removing something that's already that way is a
  quiet success with no journal entry (a double tap or two people at once).
- **Overdue** means a due date before the household's today (D11), worked out on every read.
- **The journal** shows names, quantities, assignees, due dates and priorities, but not notes or
  descriptions (only that one changed).
- **Not built:** item categories, units, recurring chores, reminders or notifications (D12,
  roadmap Phase 17), links to expenses.

### D29 — Budgets (periods and 80% confirmed; the rest proposed)

How roadmap Phase 16 (2026-11-13) implements it ([budgets.md](budgets.md)):

- **Decided by the owner:** any active member manages budgets (O3); a budget is weekly, monthly or
  quarterly; "Near limit" from 80%.
- **Periods:** weeks Monday to Sunday (D25), calendar months and calendar quarters, in the
  household's timezone (D11).
- **One active budget** per household, category (or everything) and period: a monthly and a weekly
  budget for groceries can both exist, two monthly ones can't (`HJ032`).
- **No names, notes or custom date ranges:** a budget is named by what it covers and its period
  ("Monthly budget for groceries").
- **Starts** with the period it's added in; it doesn't cover earlier ones.
- **A new limit** applies from the current period on; periods that have ended keep the limit they
  had, so their figures can always be worked out again from the records.
- **Removing** a budget stops it covering the current period and later ones; periods that had ended
  keep it. It can't be changed afterwards, and a new one can be added for the same category and
  period.
- **Statuses:** "Nothing spent yet", "On track", "Near limit" (80% or more, up to and including the
  limit) and "Over budget" (more than the limit). Overspending is shown as an amount over, never as a
  negative remainder.
- **No rollover** (O11, open). Nothing is blocked when a budget is reached, and nothing is sent
  (notifications are D12's, roadmap Phase 17).
